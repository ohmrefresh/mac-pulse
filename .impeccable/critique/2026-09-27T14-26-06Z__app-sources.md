Evidence: the app built from HEAD (7db37fa) and driven live — menu-bar item, popover, and 10 dashboard surfaces captured on screen in Dark appearance (Overview, Performance, Network, Processes, Developer, Storage, Battery, Sensors, Timeline, Alerts, Diagnostics sheet), plus the full Swift source. Light appearance was verified on screen in a second pass, which is where the P0 surfaced.

## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Does this look AI-generated? No.** This is the rare case where the answer is a clean no, and the reason is specific: the app owns no typeface, no palette and no custom chrome, and its identity comes from what it refuses to show. Every shared absolute ban is respected — no side-stripe borders, no gradient text, no glassmorphism, no tiny uppercase eyebrows, no numbered section scaffolding. Grep confirms zero `shadow`, `blur` or `material` in the entire UI layer; gradients appear only as chart area fills, the app icon, and one capsule.

The product-register bar is "would a user fluent in Activity Monitor, iStat Menus and Raycast trust this, or pause at every subtly-off component." They would mostly trust it — and pause in three places: the Sensors page (a component vocabulary that exists nowhere else in the app), the Alerts rows (steppers floating ~600 px from the values they drive), and the dash placeholders that contradict the app's own stated honesty rule.

It also breaks its own written Don'ts in six places I verified in source: a `.regularMaterial` blur on the chart tooltip (`HistoryChart.swift:174`) against "no shadows, blur or glass"; three foreign hues — `.cyan`, `.orange`, `.purple` — on the one latency family (`DetailViews.swift:380`) when `MetricStyle.internet.shade(_:of:)` exists for exactly that; a filled card inside a card for every fan row (`SensorsView.swift:317`); status colour used for routine variation in `DeltaLabel` (`Components.swift:313`, an orange arrow for a 0.6 % tick); `withAnimation(.snappy)` on the sensors disclosure (`SensorsView.swift:228`) with no Reduce Motion path, against "the app currently ships no motion"; and `title.uppercased()` in the Diagnostics sheet (`DiagnosticsView.swift:85`) against the sentence-case rule.

The one layout that reads generic is the **Overview grid**: six same-sized cards, each icon + title + badge + big figure + sparkline + three footer stats. It is the app's front door and it treats a Warning-state Memory card exactly like four Healthy ones apart from a small badge. For a product whose promise is "what is happening, when did it start, what is likely causing it", the front door currently answers only the first third, and answers it with equal emphasis everywhere.

## Overall Impression

This is an instrument, and it mostly behaves like one: dense, unemotional, timestamped, native. The craft in the *readings* is real — monospaced digits, min/max bands, "gaps are timeouts", a diagnostics sheet that separates OBSERVED from POSSIBLE CAUSE and hedges only the cause.

The gap is between the product's stated principles and what four of the ten pages actually render. The document says "a value this Mac cannot report is absent, never dashed"; the Sensors page prints `— —` four times above the fold and the Developer table prints `–` twenty times. The document says one component vocabulary; the app has three range pickers, two empty-state styles and a Sensors page built from parts used nowhere else. **The single biggest opportunity is to make the interface obey its own design system** — the system is already written down and it is a good one.

## What's Working

1. **Refusals are visible and specific.** "Temperatures and fans use undocumented macOS interfaces and may be unavailable after a macOS update." "Off — asks 1.1.1.1 when enabled" on the Public IP toggle. "Causes are suggestions, not certainties." Most monitors bluff here; this one names its own limits in the place the limit applies, which is exactly what buys trust when a red state finally appears.
2. **The popover is the best-designed surface in the app.** Five rows, each metric → value → sparkline → health badge, then Top Processes, then one button with its shortcut printed on it (`Open Mac Pulse ⌘O`). It answers the glance question without a window, which is the product's central claim, and it shows root processes (WindowServer at 28.8 %) that an unprivileged app normally cannot reach.
3. **Charts are honest instruments, not decoration.** Per-core lines stepped in one hue, a stats rail beside the plot instead of a legend soup, "Total plus 15 logical cores" as a caption, real axis units, and the min/max band on stored ranges. The Performance page in particular reads like a tool, not a dashboard template.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The entire Health Level colour vocabulary fails WCAG AA in Light appearance

- **Why it matters**: `HealthBadge` (`Formatting.swift:121`) draws its text in `level.tint` over *the same tint at 15 %* — same-hue ink on same-hue fill. Over the light card surface that measures **green ≈ 1.8:1, orange ≈ 1.8:1, red ≈ 2.7:1** (computed from the composited fill; caption-weight text needs 4.5:1). Verified on screen: in Light, the Healthy and Warning badges are pale text on a pale wash. Dark passes, which is why it shipped — the mockup is dark. This is the app's whole status language, and the same pairing drives the delta arrows (`Components.swift:313`), the timeline severity dot (`AlertsView.swift:162`, the row's only severity signal) and the Sensors thermal banner (`SensorsView.swift:77`), all below the 3:1 non-text floor in Light.
- PRODUCT.md claims the contrast floors are "asserted by tests, so a palette change that drops below them fails the build". The tests (`App/Tests/AppSettingsTests.swift:124–153`) cover only `OKLCH.shade()` ramps at 3:1. The status colours have no test at all.
- **Fix**: keep the 15 % fill, darken the ink per appearance (`OKLCH.shade(of:index:count:dark:)` already does this for metric ramps), and add a test asserting 4.5:1 for badge text and 3:1 for arrow/dot/banner in both appearances.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable audit App/Sources`

### [P1] The app breaks its own "never show a value we don't measure" rule, in public

- **Why it matters**: This is Design Principle 1 and the thing the whole product sells — a dash is indistinguishable from "the reading broke", which is precisely the ambiguity the principle exists to remove. It is not hidden: the Sensors page shows `— —` under all four tiles (CPU 40 °C, Hottest 52 °C, SSD 34 °C, Battery 31 °C) and the Developer table shows `–` in the CPU and Memory column of every one of ~10 visible container rows.
- **Fix**: Make nil render as omission everywhere, the way `StatRail` already does (`Components.swift:270`, which documents the rule). The offenders are `BigValue` (`Components.swift:240`) and `FooterStats` (`Components.swift:264`), both `?? "--"`, plus `OverviewView.swift:96,171,174,177`, `DetailViews.swift:414,466,467,468` and `ProcessesView.swift:45` (the User column). For the Docker table, drop the CPU/Memory columns when stats are unavailable (they only stream while the Developer view is visible) rather than filling them with dashes.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden App/Sources`

### [P1] The Overview has no triage order — the front door treats a warning like a healthy reading

- **Why it matters**: The user opens this because something already feels wrong. The Overview answers with six identically weighted cards in a fixed grid; when Memory was in Warning the only difference from its healthy neighbours was a small orange capsule. The user still has to scan all six and compare. The product's own promise ("what changed and when, not just what is now") is delivered only by the Recent Activity card in the bottom-right corner.
- **Fix**: Give the page a lead. Order or promote by health so an unhealthy metric takes the first slot, or add one line under the title that names the current worst state and when it started ("Memory pressure reached Warning at 21:07 — 4 minutes ago"), sourced from the timeline the app already keeps. Keep the grid for everything that is fine.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable layout App/Sources/OverviewView.swift`

### [P1] The Timeline floods, and the flood buries the signal

- **Why it matters**: In the captured hour, 7 of the 16 visible events were "Notes CPU increased 99–100 % for 10 s+" repeating every 30–60 s, all the same orange severity. The one structural event in that window — "Memory pressure → Warning" — sat between two of them, visually identical. A log that repeats itself teaches the user to stop reading it, which costs the product its "when did it start" claim.
- **Fix**: Collapse consecutive same-process, same-metric events into one row with a count and a span ("Notes CPU high — 7 times, 21:05–21:10, peak 100 %"), and require a real recovery before the next event of the same kind is emitted. This lives in `TimelineConfig`'s heuristics (internal by design, per CLAUDE.md) plus the row rendering in `TimelineSectionView.swift`, not in Settings.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable distill App/Sources/TimelineSectionView.swift`

### [P2] One app, three range pickers and two empty-state styles

- **Why it matters**: Consistency is the product register's core virtue — the same control should mean the same thing on every page. Today: Performance and Network use a segmented `Live · 1h · 6h · 24h · 7d · 30d` at top right; Sensors uses `1h · 6h · 24h · 7d` (no Live, no 30 d) under a health panel; Timeline uses a labelled `Range` row with an extra `Category` popup, left-aligned. Empty states split the same way — `ContentUnavailableView` on Disk/Timeline/Diagnostics, bare secondary text on Alerts and Sensors. Sensors additionally introduces tinted rounded-square icon chips, a large bordered health panel instead of the capsule `HealthBadge`, and the only page-title icon in the app.
- **Fix**: Promote one `ChartRange` picker and one empty-state component into the shared kit and use them everywhere, and rebuild the Sensors header out of `PageHeader` + `MetricCard` + `HealthBadge` like every other page. Where Sensors genuinely needs fewer ranges, disable the missing ones rather than shipping a different control.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable extract App/Sources`

### [P2] Alerts rows are a 7-control line with broken spatial logic

- **Why it matters**: Each rule row carries a toggle, an editable name, a severity picker, a delete button, a metric label, a comparator picker, a threshold field, a unit and a duration stepper — well past the 4-item working-memory limit, all at the same visual weight, times eight rows. Worse, inside `.formStyle(.grouped)` the labelled `Stepper` (`AlertsView.swift:113`) pushes its control to the far right edge, so the ↕ that changes "30 s" sits ~600 px away from it. And `.disabled(!rule.isEnabled)` (`AlertsView.swift:117`) covers only the condition row, so a disabled rule renders half-dim: name and severity look live, the condition looks dead.
- **Fix**: Put the stepper next to its value (`Stepper(value:in:step:) { }` with the text drawn inline, or `.labelsHidden()` in a `.fixedSize()` HStack), move `.disabled` to the whole `VStack` so an off rule reads as one off thing, and collapse the condition line into a sentence the user reads left to right ("CPU above 90 % for 30 s") that expands to controls on click.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable layout App/Sources/AlertsView.swift`

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (impatient power user)**: ⌘1–⌘4 cover four of ten sections; Sensors, Timeline, Alerts, Developer, Storage and Battery need the mouse or Tab-then-arrows (`DashboardView.swift:37`). ⌘K advertises search but only opens Processes. The Diagnostics recommendation says "see Processes, sorted by Memory" but nothing carries the user there — the table does sort by any header (`ProcessesView.swift:24`), yet the recommendation is not a link and does not pre-sort, so the user re-does the work by hand. The Docker table lists 77 containers alphabetically with the one *running* container 1st only by luck of the alphabet, and its Status column truncates ("Exited (1) 4 months a…").

**Sam (accessibility-dependent)**: eight view files contain zero accessibility annotations — `PopoverView`, `SensorsView` (421 lines), `ProcessesView`, `AlertsView`, `DiagnosticsView`, `SettingsView`, `DeveloperView`, `TimelineSectionView` — so the popover (the app's primary surface), the sensor table and the alert rows rely entirely on SwiftUI defaults, while `HistoryChart` and `TimeSeriesChart` do the summarised-chart work properly. System Events reported 0 AX windows while the popover was open, which needs a real VoiceOver pass to confirm or dismiss. The Alerts page carries meaning in a half-dimmed row; the fan bars carry "0 %" in a bar whose value is also printed, which is correct.

**P2, DevOps / SRE (from PRODUCT.md)**: the Network page is the app's strongest answer for this persona — three probe targets, packet loss per target, DNS resolver and lookup time, gaps drawn as timeouts. But the incident question ("when did it start") routes to a Timeline that is currently 44 % one noisy process, and there is no way to filter the timeline to *network* events without the Category popup that only exists on that page. Alert rules ship disabled, so an SRE who installs this and walks away is notified of nothing, and no screen says so.

**P3, power user (from PRODUCT.md)**: Sensors is the page they came for and it is the page that speaks the least English — `PMU tdie8`, `PMU tcal`, `PMU tdie13`, 17 of them, sorted "Highest first", with `— —` under the summary tiles and a "Min / Max since launch" caption sitting under a 1 h range picker that implies the numbers are windowed. Battery, by contrast, is exemplary for this persona: State, Condition, Cycle count, Maximum capacity, plus an honest "No Bluetooth accessories reporting a battery level."

## Minor Observations

- **Processes "Started" truncates under a non-English locale**: `26 Sep 2569 BE…`. Rendering the date in the system locale and calendar is correct (Activity Monitor does the same) — the bug is a column too narrow for it. Use a relative/compact format for anything older than today, or let the column size to its content.
- **Developer shows a spinner** while listening ports load; the design system asks for a skeleton, never a spinner ("Loading…" text on Sensors is closer to the rule).
- **Low-content pages leave 50–60 % of the canvas empty** (Battery ends a third of the way down; the Diagnostics sheet is a fixed 620×520 pt holding one finding card). Either let the content breathe deliberately or let the sheet size to its findings.
- **Menu-bar item is 405 px wide** with five segments and four `|` separators at the default settings (` CPU 12% |  MEM 80% |  ↓349K ↑13K |  7ms |  42°C`). On a 13″ screen with a notch that is a lot of bar; a tighter default (two or three segments) would leave room for the system's own items.
- **Alert thresholds accept any number** with no bounds or validation copy — 9000 % CPU is enterable.
- **Light appearance was not verified on screen** in this run; both appearances are a stated design requirement.
- **`Text("1 second"/"\(n) seconds")` is hand-pluralised** in `SettingsView.swift:33` — fine while the app is English-only, wrong the moment it is not, and the app already renders locale-formatted dates.

## Questions to Consider

- If the Overview could show only one thing when something is wrong, what would it be — and what would the page look like if that were the design instead of a grid?
- The Timeline is the product's differentiator. What would it take for a user to read it top-to-bottom and come away with a story rather than a log?
- Alerts ship disabled for a good reason. What is the smallest honest nudge that gets a user to turn on the two rules that matter to them, without becoming onboarding?
- Sensors is the only page that invented its own components. Was that because the page needed something the system lacks — and if so, what belongs in the system?
- The app refuses to show what it cannot measure. Should it also refuse to show what it *can* measure but that carries no information right now (a flat 0 RPM fan, a 0 KB/s network), or is presence itself the signal?
